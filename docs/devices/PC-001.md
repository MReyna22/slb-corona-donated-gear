## Evidence and limits

The hardware and virtualization observations were originally transcribed from user-supplied BIOS photographs IMG_9479 through IMG_9483. Two source photographs, IMG_9479 and IMG_9480, were recovered on October 5, 2026 and published below as permanent, metadata-free redactions. They support the BIOS version, memory, processor, and detected-device observations. Their original capture dates were not independently established.

IMG_9481 through IMG_9483 and the additional boot-menu, SATA Operation, and TPM photographs have not yet been recovered. UEFI, Secure Boot, virtualization, SATA Operation, and TPM settings remain prior transcribed observations; the two recovered photographs do not independently show those settings. Device identifiers are excluded from this record.

BIOS detection confirms the listed hardware and settings were visible; it does not establish storage health, sustained stability, or successful VM operation. The operator reported that pre-boot diagnostics passed without errors displayed. No diagnostics-result screenshot or test-scope details were supplied.

The eight new screenshots are supplied test evidence; no device commands were executed during this archival review. The Windows Update screenshot reports "You're up to date" and "Last checked: Today, 8:48 PM"; its filename dates the capture October 5, 2026. This documents the displayed update state then, rather than an audit of all optional drivers, BIOS firmware, or future updates. Basic Windows storage status is not a comprehensive SMART, surface, endurance, or performance test.

The wired screenshot shows Ethernet Up and Wi-Fi Disconnected alongside successful ping/DNS results. The Wi-Fi screenshots show the Qualcomm QCA9377 Up and Ethernet Disconnected alongside the later ping/DNS results. Negotiated adapter rates are not measured throughput. DNS AAAA answers do not prove public IPv6 connectivity. The browser screenshot was supplied during the Wi-Fi test sequence, but it alone does not identify the active egress adapter.

The operator reports that no 2.5-inch SATA drive is installed. The labels identify the wireless card as Dell DW1810 and the SSD as Toshiba KBG40ZNS128G, 128 GB. Subsequent screenshots support basic Windows storage status and network checks, and reassembly/startup are operator-confirmed. Cooling performance remains untested.

These are published component specifications, not measured PC-001 throughput or proof that each capability was tested.
